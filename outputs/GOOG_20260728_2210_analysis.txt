# Alphabet Inc. (GOOG) — Value Analysis

---

## 1. What the Three Scenarios Tell Us

The three scenarios paint a consistent and unambiguous picture: **at $327, Alphabet is trading significantly above intrinsic value across every case.**

| Scenario | Intrinsic Value | Price/IV Ratio | Zone |
|----------|----------------|----------------|------|
| Bear | $67 | 4.85x | CONSIDER TRIMMING |
| Base | $117 | 2.80x | CONSIDER TRIMMING |
| Bull | $198 | 1.65x | CONSIDER TRIMMING |

Even in the most optimistic scenario — where Alphabet's moat widens, AI investments pay off handsomely, and FCF growth reaccelerates toward the 12.5% revenue CAGR [3][4] — the stock still trades at **1.65x intrinsic value**. You are paying $1.65 for every $1.00 of estimated value in the best case. In the base case, you are paying $2.80 per dollar of value.

This is the inverse of everything the margin-of-safety framework demands [1][2]. The framework calls for paying **≤70 cents per dollar** for an aggressive buy signal. Here, the market is charging a 65–385% premium above value depending on assumptions.

The spread between the bear IV ($67) and bull IV ($198) is also notably wide — a **3x range** — which itself signals meaningful uncertainty, particularly around the $91.4B TTM capex program. When uncertainty is high, the required margin of safety should be *larger*, not smaller [1].

---

## 2. Would Buffett Buy, Hold, or Avoid?

**Buffett would avoid adding at this price. Existing holders should consider trimming.**

The reasoning is direct and grounded in [2]: every scenario places the stock well above the 95–100¢ zone where the instruction is simply "hold, do not add." At ratios of 1.65x to 4.85x intrinsic value, this stock sits in territory where the framework says *reduce exposure*.

The business quality is not in question. Alphabet's moat — search network effects, brand dominance, AI infrastructure, and scale advantages — is genuine and well-documented [3][5]. A 48.7% ROE confirms the underlying economics are exceptional. But as Buffett has repeatedly demonstrated, a wonderful business at a terrible price is still a terrible investment. Paying 2.80x intrinsic value in the base case offers no margin of safety whatsoever — it offers the *opposite*: a near-certain margin of loss [1].

The 5-year FCF CAGR of 6.9% meaningfully lagging the 12.5% revenue CAGR also signals that heavy capex is absorbing cash that does not yet show up as owner earnings. Until that investment cycle matures, the gap between accounting optimism and free cash reality warrants extra caution.

---

## 3. What Would Need to Change to Enter Buy Territory

Using the cents-per-dollar framework [2], a price of **≤70¢ per $1 of intrinsic value** is the aggressive buy threshold. Working backward from the scenarios:

| Scenario | IV | Buy Price (≤70¢) | Acceptable Buy (≤80¢) |
|----------|----|------------------|----------------------|
| Bear | $67 | ≤$47 | ≤$54 |
| Base | $117 | ≤$82 | ≤$94 |
| Bull | $198 | ≤$139 | ≤$158 |

**For a disciplined value buyer, the base-case buy price is approximately $82–$94.** That represents a **72–75% decline from the current $327 price** — a gap so large it suggests the market is pricing in sustained hyper-growth that the FCF history does not yet support.

What *could* close this gap over time without a price decline:

1. **Capex normalization** — If the $91.4B investment cycle matures and FCF conversion improves sharply, owner earnings could re-rate toward or beyond the bull case, lifting intrinsic value
2. **AI monetization inflection** — Demonstrated, durable revenue contribution from AI products that expands the moat [5] rather than merely defending it
