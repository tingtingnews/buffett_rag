# TSMC (TSM) — Three-Scenario Analysis

---

## 1. What the Three Scenarios Tell Us

The scenario spread reveals a stock where **the base and bull cases are compelling but the bear case demands respect**.

| Scenario | Owner Earnings | IV | Price/IV | Zone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | $71.7B | $319 | 1.35x | Consider Trimming |
| Base | $85.2B | $620 | 0.69x | **IDEAL** |
| Bull | $85.2B | $752 | 0.57x | **IDEAL** |

The 2-to-1 split tells a clear story: **if your base assumptions hold, TSMC at $430 is genuinely cheap.** At 69¢ per $1 of intrinsic value, it sits squarely in the "buy aggressively" zone defined in the framework [2]. The bull case at 57¢ is even more attractive.

However, the bear case at $319 IV — where price exceeds intrinsic value by 35% — is not a fringe scenario to dismiss. It reflects what happens when you apply a growth haircut and a higher discount rate to a business with **real geopolitical concentration risk** (one island, one fab network). The 11% discount rate used in the base case is already the *highest in the portfolio*, signaling that this risk is explicitly priced into the thinking [4].

The 20% historical revenue and FCF CAGR over five years, combined with 30% ROE, provides strong empirical support for the base case assumptions. These are not wishful numbers — they are documented performance [5].

---

## 2. Would Buffett Buy, Hold, or Avoid at $430?

**Buffett would most likely buy a measured position, not avoid — but not bet the farm either.**

Here is the reasoning:

The margin of safety principle requires buying *meaningfully below* intrinsic value so that even if your estimate is somewhat wrong, you are protected from permanent loss [1]. In the base case, TSMC at $430 offers a **31% margin of safety** — well inside the ≤70¢ ideal threshold [2]. That is not a thin margin; that is a genuine cushion.

TSMC also has the characteristics Buffett prizes: an irreplaceable moat (≈90% of advanced chip manufacturing), pricing power embedded in customer dependency, and a natural "arms dealer" position that benefits regardless of which chip designer wins the AI race [4]. Nvidia, Apple, AMD, Broadcom — they all need TSMC. That is a durable competitive advantage.

**The hesitation** comes from the bear case. Buffett's bridge analogy is instructive [1]: if the bear case IV is $319 and current price is $430, a deterioration in assumptions does not just shrink your return — it produces a real loss. For Buffett, geopolitical risk (Taiwan Strait) is not easily modeled in a DCF, and he has historically avoided businesses where a single exogenous event could impair the entire asset base.

**Verdict: Buy at current price, but sized appropriately for the geopolitical tail risk. Not a maximum-conviction position unless the bear case improves.**

---

## 3. What Would Need to Change to Enter Stronger Buy Territory?

Two paths exist:

**A. Price declines toward the bear/base midpoint (~$350–370)**
At ~$350, even the bear case IV of $319 represents only a modest overpay, while the base case margin of safety widens to ~44%. That risk/reward would justify a more aggressive position under the ≤70¢ framework [2].

**B. Bear case assumptions improve**
The bear case is driven by a growth haircut and higher discount rate [5]. If TSMC demonstrates sustained capex execution, geographic diversification (Arizona fabs ramping), and geopolitical de-escalation, a higher bear-case floor would be warranted. That would compress the scenario spread and increase conviction.

**C. Nothing needs to change for a base-case buyer**
At 69¢ on the dollar today [2], a disciplined investor comfortable with the base assumptions already has a buy signal. The question is position sizing, not whether to own it at all.
