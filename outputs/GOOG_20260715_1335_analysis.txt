# Alphabet Inc. (GOOG) — Value-Investing Scenario Analysis

---

## 1. What the Three Scenarios Tell Us

The scenario spread reveals a stock that is **priced for perfection in its base case and significantly overvalued in its bear case**, with only the most optimistic outcome justifying the current price.

| Scenario | IV | Price/IV Ratio | Zone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | $150 | 2.39x | Consider Trimming |
| Base | $260 | 1.37x | Consider Trimming |
| Bull | $476 | 0.75x | Acceptable |

The critical observation: **at $357, you need the bull case to simply break even on margin of safety**. Even there, per Card [2], a 0.75x ratio (75¢ per $1 of intrinsic value) sits at the outer edge of the "good" zone — not a screaming buy. In both the bear and base scenarios, the market is already pricing in more than $1 of value for every dollar paid, meaning **there is no margin of safety in two of three plausible futures** [1][2].

The wide gap between the bear IV ($150) and bull IV ($476) — a 3.2x spread — signals **genuine fundamental uncertainty**, driven primarily by whether Alphabet's $91.4B TTM capex converts into high-return AI-driven growth or becomes a drag on owner earnings [1]. The 6.9% historical FCF CAGR lagging well behind the 12.5% revenue CAGR already suggests **capital intensity is compressing free cash flow conversion** — a real risk that the base and bear cases rightfully penalize.

The ROE of 38.9% confirms the underlying business quality is exceptional. The moat — search network effects, brand, cloud scale, YouTube — is real and durable [3][5]. **The problem is not the business. The problem is the price.**

---

## 2. Would Buffett Buy, Hold, or Avoid?

**Buffett would not buy at $357. Most likely he would hold an existing position cautiously, but would not add.**

Here is the reasoning, drawn directly from the framework:

Per Card [1], the margin of safety principle requires buying *meaningfully below* intrinsic value so that even an imperfect estimate leaves you protected from permanent loss. At current prices, you are paying **1.37x the base intrinsic value** — you are not driving a 10-ton truck across a 30-ton bridge; you are driving a 48-ton truck across it.

Per Card [2], the cents-per-dollar framework is explicit: a ratio above 95¢–100¢ means "hold, do not add," and above 100¢ signals overvaluation. At 1.37x in the base case, Alphabet sits **well into territory where the framework says do not add**.

Only in the bull scenario does the ratio (0.75x) reach the "acceptable" zone. But Buffett and Munger have consistently warned against anchoring to optimistic cases. Paying full bull-case prices while bearing bear-case risk is precisely the asymmetry disciplined value investors avoid [1][2].

The 38.9% ROE and durable moat [3][5] would earn Buffett's admiration for the *business*. But admiring a business and buying its stock at any price are two very different things — a distinction Buffett has made explicitly throughout his career.

> **Verdict: Do not buy. Do not add. Hold only if already owned with a lower cost basis.**
