# Microsoft (MSFT) — Value Analysis at $497

---

## 1. What the Three Scenarios Tell Us

The three scenarios paint a consistent and sobering picture: **MSFT is expensive at every reasonable level of analysis.**

| Scenario | Owner Earnings | Intrinsic Value | Price/IV Ratio | Zone |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|----------------|------|
| Bear | $72.9B | $143 | 3.48x | Consider Trimming |
| Base | $92.9B | $256 | 1.94x | Consider Trimming |
| Bull | $109.2B | $447 | 1.11x | Do Not Buy |

The most striking data point here is this: **even in the bull case — where Azure RPO growth of 99% YoY is fully credited, AI margins expand as compute costs fall, and the full 75% growth-capex add-back is assumed — the stock still trades above its intrinsic value at $447 vs. a $497 price.** [Card 5, Bull reasoning]

The base case, which uses a 10.5% discount rate and a modest haircut to the historical 16.1% revenue CAGR, produces an IV of just $256 — meaning the market is currently pricing MSFT at **nearly twice its most probable intrinsic value.** [Card 5, Base reasoning]

The bear case is genuinely alarming: if the 75% growth-capex add-back assumption fails and reverts to 50%, IV collapses toward $103–$143, implying a potential **70%+ drawdown from current prices** in an adverse scenario. [Card 5, Bear reasoning]

The FCF CAGR divergence is also a red flag worth flagging explicitly: while revenue has compounded at 16.1% over five years, **FCF has grown at only 4.0%.** [Card 5, Historical data] This gap — likely driven by the $190B/year capex cycle — is precisely the uncertainty that makes the growth-capex add-back assumption so consequential.

---

## 2. Would Buffett Buy, Hold, or Avoid?

**Buffett would avoid adding at $497, and would likely consider trimming an existing position.**

The margin-of-safety framework is unambiguous here [2]: the ideal buy zone requires paying **≤70 cents per $1 of intrinsic value.** Even using the most optimistic bull-case IV of $447, the current price of $497 represents paying **$1.11 for every $1 of intrinsic value** — you are *paying a premium* to own it, with no margin of safety whatsoever.

At the base-case IV of $256, you are paying approximately **$1.94 per $1 of intrinsic value.** That is not a margin of safety; it is the *opposite* of one.

Buffett's bridge analogy [1] makes this vivid: if the bridge holds 30 tons, you drive 10-ton trucks across it. At current prices, you would be driving a *50-ton truck* across a bridge rated for 26 tons (the bull case IV). The consequences of being wrong are severe and asymmetric.

The probability-weighted expected return of approximately **7.5%** [4] — meaningfully below the MCO (~13.1%) and Visa (~11.8%) alternatives cited in the same card — further confirms this is not a compelling capital allocation decision relative to other opportunities.

---

## 3. What Would Need to Change for MSFT to Enter Buy Territory?

For MSFT to reach the **acceptable buy zone (≤80¢ per $1 of IV)** using the base-case IV of $256, the price would need to fall to approximately **$205 or below** — a decline of roughly **59% from current levels.**

For the more lenient **cautious accumulation zone (≤95¢)**, the price would need to reach approximately **$243** — still a ~51% decline.

Even anchoring generously to the bull-case IV of $447, a ≤70¢ buy signal would require a price of approximately **$313** — still a ~37% decline from $497.

**Alternatively**, intrinsic value itself could rise to meet the price — but this would require:
- Sustained confirmation that the growth-capex add-back assumption holds at 75% or higher
