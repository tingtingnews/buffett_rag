# Microsoft (MSFT) — Value Analysis at $394

---

## 1. What the Three Scenarios Tell Us

The three scenarios paint a picture of a **high-quality business trading at a demanding price**:

| Scenario | Owner Earnings | Intrinsic Value | Price/IV Ratio | Zone |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|----------------|------|
| Bear | $75.6B | $159 | 2.47x | Consider Trimming |
| Base | $86.7B | $279 | 1.41x | Consider Trimming |
| Bull | $95.8B | $525 | 0.75x | Acceptable |

The critical insight is the **enormous spread between bear ($159) and bull ($525)**. This spread is driven almost entirely by one variable: whether the 75% growth-capex add-back assumption holds [Card 5]. If Microsoft's $190B/year capex investment fails to generate the expected returns, intrinsic value collapses dramatically. The bull case requires near-peak Azure growth (the $627B RPO supporting this [Card 5]) *and* full validation of the capex efficiency assumption. You are essentially paying $394 today and betting heavily that the bull case materializes.

The probability-weighted expected return of approximately **7.5%** [Card 4] — well below the 12–15% target benchmark — confirms that the market has already priced in considerable optimism.

---

## 2. Would Buffett Buy, Hold, or Avoid at $394?

**Buffett would hold existing positions but not add, and would likely avoid initiating a new position.**

Here is the reasoning:

The margin-of-safety framework [Cards 1, 2, 3] is unambiguous. The cents-per-dollar zones require:
- **≤75¢ to buy aggressively** (bull case barely touches this at 75¢)
- **≤80¢ to buy normally**
- **>100¢ signals avoid or trim**

At $394, you are paying **$1.41 per $1.00 of base-case intrinsic value**. Even using the base case generously, this is deep in "Consider Trimming" territory [Card 5]. The *only* scenario where MSFT clears the acceptable buy threshold (≤80¢) is the full bull case — and relying solely on the bull case to justify a purchase violates the core margin-of-safety principle [Card 1]: *"even if your estimate is somewhat wrong, you are protected from permanent loss."* At current prices, being even modestly wrong on the bull case means significant overpayment.

Buffett's bridge analogy is apt here [Card 1]: the bridge might hold 525 tons in the best case, but you cannot ignore the scenario where it holds only 159.

---

## 3. What Would Need to Change to Enter Buy Territory

For MSFT to enter **acceptable buy territory (≤80¢ on base case)**, the price would need to fall to approximately **$223 or below** (80% × $279 base IV).

For **aggressive buy territory (≤70¢)**, the target price would be approximately **$195 or below**.

Alternatively, the *intrinsic value itself* could rise to meet the price if:

1. **Capex efficiency is confirmed** — sustained evidence that the 75% growth-capex add-back is conservative, pushing base-case owner earnings above $86.7B
2. **Azure RPO converts to revenue** — the $627B RPO [Card 5] translating into accelerating FCF growth, closing the gap between the 3.2% historical FCF CAGR and the 12.4% revenue CAGR
3. **AI margin expansion materializes** — Goldman's thesis on falling compute costs [Card 5] reducing capex drag and lifting free cash flow structurally
4. **A meaningful market correction** — broad de-rating that brings high-quality compounders like MSFT to prices reflecting base-case rather than bull-case outcomes
