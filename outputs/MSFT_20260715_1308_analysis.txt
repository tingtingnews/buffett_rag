# Microsoft (MSFT) — Value Analysis at $385

---

## 1. What the Three Scenarios Tell Us

The three scenarios paint a consistently cautious picture, with meaningful spread in outcomes:

| Scenario | Owner Earnings | Intrinsic Value | Price/IV | Zone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | $75.6B | $149 | 2.59x | Consider Trimming |
| Base | $86.7B | $239 | 1.61x | Consider Trimming |
| Bull | $95.8B | $473 | 0.81x | Accumulate Cautiously |

**The critical takeaway:** At $385, you are paying full price for the bull case and a significant premium to both the bear and base cases. The only scenario where $385 is even remotely justifiable is the bull case — and even there, you're paying 81¢ per $1 of intrinsic value, which per the framework [2] sits in the "accumulate cautiously" zone, not a confident buy signal.

The **wide dispersion** between bear ($149) and bull ($473) is itself a warning. It reflects genuine uncertainty around two variables called out explicitly in the scenario reasoning:

- Whether the **growth-capex add-back assumption holds** (Card 5.4 warns IV collapses toward ~$103 if the add-back fails by 50%)
- Whether **$190B/year in capex** translates into durable earnings power or becomes a permanent drag

The **FCF CAGR of 3.2% versus revenue CAGR of 12.4%** is a glaring divergence that the base case explicitly haircuts for. Revenue growth without corresponding free cash flow growth is a red flag in the owner-earnings framework — you are buying *reported* growth, not *owner* growth.

---

## 2. Would Buffett Buy, Hold, or Avoid?

**Verdict: Hold existing positions. Do not add. Do not initiate a new position.**

Here is the reasoning grounded in the cards:

**On margin of safety [1][2]:** Buffett's foundational principle — borrowed from Graham — is that you only drive a 10-ton truck across a 30-ton bridge. At $385, the base case intrinsic value is $239. You are not driving a 10-ton truck. You are driving a 385-ton truck across a 239-ton bridge. That is not a margin of safety — that is paying for perfection.

Per the cents-per-dollar framework [2]:
- Bear case: paying **$2.59 per $1 of value** — deeply overvalued
- Base case: paying **$1.61 per $1 of value** — overvalued
- Bull case: paying **$0.81 per $1 of value** — only here does it approach the "accumulate cautiously" threshold

For a *new* position, Buffett would demand ≤80¢ on the **base case**, not merely on the bull case. At $385, you must be right about the bull case *just to get to acceptable*. That is not how value investing works.

**On the probability-weighted expected return [4]:** MSFT's expected return as documented is approximately **7.5%** — the lowest of the three stocks cited (MCO ≈ 13.1%, V ≈ 11.8%). Even after the $627B RPO confirmation raised the base-case probability, the expected return remains modest. Buffett has repeatedly stated he needs a compelling reason to deploy capital at lower expected returns when alternatives exist.

**On the moat [Card 5, the scenario data]:** The bull case acknowledges Azure RPO growth of +99% YoY and AI margins expanding as compute costs fall 60–70%/year. The moat is real — network effects, switching costs, and enterprise entrenchment are genuine. But **a great business at a bad price is still a bad investment.** Buffett has said this many times, most famously about his early Berkshire textile mistake.
