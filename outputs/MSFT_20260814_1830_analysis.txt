# MSFT Valuation Analysis — Buffett Framework

---

## 1. What the Three Scenarios Tell Us

The three scenarios paint a consistent and sobering picture:

| Scenario | Owner Earnings | Intrinsic Value | Price/IV Ratio | Zone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | $64.0B | $157 | 3.17x | Consider Trimming |
| Base | $111.5B | $445 | 1.12x | Do Not Buy |
| Bull | $111.5B | $541 | 0.92x | Accumulate Cautiously |

**The critical takeaway:** Even in the bull case — where optimistic growth assumptions are fully realized — MSFT trades at 92¢ per $1 of intrinsic value. You need everything to go *right* just to reach the lowest acceptable zone. In the base case, you are paying $1.12 for every $1 of intrinsic value, meaning you are already pricing in perfection and then some. The bear case is genuinely alarming at 3.17x intrinsic value — suggesting significant downside if growth disappoints.

The probability-weighted expected return of approximately **7.5%** [4] further confirms this: acceptable, but not compelling by Buffett standards, and well below the 12–15% target range [3].

---

## 2. Would Buffett Buy, Hold, or Avoid?

**Avoid adding. Do not buy.**

The margin-of-safety framework [2] is unambiguous here:

- **≤70¢** = Buy aggressively
- **≤80¢** = Acceptable to buy
- **80–95¢** = Accumulate cautiously
- **95–100¢** = Hold, do not add
- **>100¢** = Do not buy

At **1.12x intrinsic value in the base case**, MSFT sits firmly in "Do Not Buy" territory. The bull case at 0.92x barely reaches "Accumulate Cautiously" — and that requires assuming the most optimistic scenario materializes.

Buffett's bridge analogy [1] applies directly: if the bridge (your intrinsic value estimate) holds 30 tons, you don't drive a 33-ton truck across it because you *hope* the engineer was conservative. At current prices, MSFT offers no margin of safety in the base case and only a thin cushion in the bull case.

> *"Even if your estimate is somewhat wrong, you are protected from permanent loss."* [1] — At these prices, there is no such protection.

---

## 3. What Would Need to Change for a Buy Signal?

For MSFT to enter **acceptable buy territory (≤80¢ on base case)**, the price would need to fall to approximately **$356 or below** ($445 × 0.80). For the *ideal* buy zone (≤70¢), the target price would be approximately **$312 or below**.

Alternatively, the thesis could improve if:

- **Owner earnings expand materially beyond the base case** — e.g., Azure and Copilot monetization accelerates faster than modeled, pushing base-case IV significantly higher. The $627B RPO confirmation already lifted the base-case probability and raised the expected return to 7.5% [4], but this still wasn't enough to move the needle into buy territory.
- **The discount rate environment shifts** — lower long-term rates would mechanically expand intrinsic value estimates.
- **A meaningful market correction** brings price down to intrinsic value or below, without a corresponding deterioration in the business fundamentals (ROE 30%, 15% revenue and FCF CAGR) [5].

The *business quality* is not in question — 30% ROE and 15% five-year CAGRs reflect a genuine, durable moat [5]. The problem is entirely **price paid relative to value**.
