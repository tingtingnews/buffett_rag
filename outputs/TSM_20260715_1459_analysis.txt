# TSMC (TSM) — Value Analysis
## Price: $420 | Base IV: $364 | Bull IV: $602 | Bear IV: $136

---

## 1. What the Three Scenarios Tell Us

The three scenarios paint a strikingly wide picture, which itself is informative:

**Bear Case ($136 IV, ratio 3.10x):** At a 12% discount rate with impaired capex returns, TSMC is severely overvalued — the current price is *more than three times* intrinsic value. This scenario is anchored by the geopolitical risk premium already embedded in the base case [4], plus the warning that AI-speculation stocks carry a 55–65% probability of a 50%+ crash within three years. At $420, the bear case offers zero margin of safety and suggests potential permanent loss of capital.

**Base Case ($364 IV, ratio 1.15x):** Using TSMC's own documented assumptions — g1=20%, g2=12%, terminal=4%, dr=11% — the stock trades at a 15% *premium* to intrinsic value [4]. This is the "DO NOT BUY" zone. The 11% discount rate is already the highest in the portfolio, explicitly assigned to reflect geopolitical risk [4]. The business quality is not in question; the price is.

**Bull Case ($602 IV, ratio 0.70x):** Only under the most favorable assumptions — near-historical 24% FCF CAGR sustained, moat widening, discount rate compressing — does TSMC enter what the framework calls the "IDEAL" buy zone [2][3]. The arms-dealer network effect [4] is real and powerful, but *pricing this scenario in at $420 leaves no room for error.*

**The key takeaway:** The spread between bear ($136) and bull ($602) is enormous — a 4.4x range. Wide scenario dispersion is a warning sign. It signals high uncertainty, which is precisely what the margin-of-safety principle exists to compensate for [1].

---

## 2. Would Buffett Buy, Hold, or Avoid?

**Avoid adding. Hold only if already owned with a low cost basis.**

The reasoning is straightforward from the framework:

- At $420 versus a base IV of $364, you are paying **$1.15 for every $1.00 of intrinsic value** in the base case. That is unambiguously above the threshold — the framework states greater than 100¢ on the dollar means do not buy [2].
- The margin-of-safety principle, borrowed from Benjamin Graham and central to Buffett's methodology, requires buying *meaningfully below* intrinsic value so that estimation errors are forgiven [1]. Here, even the base case estimate is below the current price.
- Buffett himself famously exited much of his TSMC position in early 2023 citing geopolitical concerns — a real-world data point consistent with the 11% discount rate rationale [4].
- The only scenario where $420 represents fair value is the bull case, and paying full price for the bull case is the antithesis of value investing. You are assuming perfection.

---

## 3. What Would Need to Change for a Buy Signal?

Using the framework's zones [2], here is the math:

| Zone | Threshold | Required Price |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptable (80¢) | Base IV × 0.80 | **≤ $291** |
| Good (75¢) | Base IV × 0.75 | **≤ $273** |
| Ideal (70¢) | Base IV × 0.70 | **≤ $255** |

**For a buy signal, TSMC would need to fall roughly 30–40% from current levels** to reach acceptable-to-good territory on the base case IV of $364.

Alternatively, the *bull case IV* ($602) would need to become the *base case* — which would require:
- Geopolitical risk materially diminishing (reducing the 11% discount rate) [4]
- Sustained FCF growth near the historical 24% CAGR [4]
- No AI-speculation correction materializing [1][4]

That is a lot of conditions to bank on before the price moves. A disciplined value investor waits for price to come to them, not the other way around.
