# MSFT Valuation Analysis — Value Investing Framework

---

## 1. What the Three Scenarios Tell Us

The three scenarios paint a consistent and sobering picture:

**Bear Case (IV = $157, ratio = 3.33x):** Even under pessimistic assumptions with growth haircuts and a higher discount rate, the bear-case intrinsic value is **$157** — meaning the current price of $521 is more than **3x the downside intrinsic value**. This is not a floor that offers comfort; it is a warning about how much optimism is already baked into the price.

**Base Case (IV = $445, ratio = 1.17x):** Under canonical, carefully calibrated assumptions with owner earnings of $111.5B, MSFT trades at **117¢ per $1 of intrinsic value**. This is meaningfully above fair value — you are paying a premium, not a discount.

**Bull Case (IV = $541, ratio = 0.96x):** Only under the most optimistic scenario — where growth sustains near its recent pace — does the price approach intrinsic value, landing at approximately **96¢ per dollar**. Even here, the stock merely enters "hold" territory, not buy territory.

The asymmetry is stark: **you need the bull case just to break even on value**, while the bear case implies catastrophic overpayment. The 15% historical revenue and FCF CAGR and 30% ROE confirm this is a magnificent business — but magnificent businesses at excessive prices are still poor investments [1].

---

**Avoid adding. Hold only if already owned.**

Applying the cents-per-dollar framework directly [2]:

At 117¢ on the base case, MSFT sits firmly in **"Do Not Buy"** territory [2]. The probability-weighted expected return of approximately **7.5%** — well below the 12–15% target range — confirms the market is pricing in substantial optimism with little room for error [4].

Buffett's own bridge analogy is instructive here [1]: if the bridge is rated for 30 tons (IV = $445), you should not drive a 52-ton truck across it (price = $521). The margin of safety is not just absent — **it is inverted**.

## 3. What Would Need to Change to Enter Buy Territory

To reach the **ideal buy zone of ≤ 70¢** on the base case intrinsic value of $445:

> **Target price ≤ ~$312**

That represents a **~40% decline from current levels** — a significant re-rating that would require either:

- A broad market correction compressing multiples
- A meaningful deterioration in near-term earnings that spooked the market (creating opportunity if the long-term thesis remained intact)
- A revision **upward** of intrinsic value through sustained owner earnings growth well beyond current base-case assumptions

For the **acceptable buy zone (≤ 80¢)**, the target would be approximately **$356** — still roughly a **32% discount to today's price**.

Alternatively, if owner earnings grew materially — say, AI-driven monetization lifted the base-case OE significantly above $111.5B — intrinsic value could rise to meet the price. But that is speculative and precisely the kind of "counting on optimism" that value discipline guards against [1][2].
